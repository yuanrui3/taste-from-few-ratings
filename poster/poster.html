1Question

Facial-beauty models predict a crowd's average score for a cropped studio face. Can a model learn one person's taste for whole people from their ordinary photos, and from how few of that person's ratings?

2Data

467 public profiles; 412 people whose face recurs in at least 4 photos (3,526 photos). One rater scored each whole person 0–10. Split frozen before any rating: 305 training, 99 test people.

6
Blendridge on the n ratings, rank-blended with the crowd at weight n / (n + 20); runs in the browser, ratings never leave the device

4What is new

  • One rater's taste for whole people in everyday photos, not a crowd's score for a studio face
  • Appearance separated and tested: scenery, clothing, photo style and photo conditions ruled out
  • Useful from 20–40 ratings, chosen before any rating, via a crowd carried over from studio faces
  • Pre-registered throughout, negative results kept

5How fast it learns your taste

Crowd start + your ratings (adopted) Your ratings alone Live re-rating check Reference

Means over 20 random splits of the training people. With zero ratings the crowd start alone reaches 0.39; forty ratings (about four minutes) reach 0.55, and the rater's live re-rating gave 0.54. Past 40, each rating adds about a quarter as much.

6It sees the person, not the photo

Removing photo-style directions leaves the score unchanged (0.595 → 0.595). Resolution, face size, framing, group photos and scene brightness explain 1.5% of a person's score variation; correcting for them moves no one.

8People the rater rarely rated

The rater's pool was mostly White and East Asian women. 160 research portraits (MEBeauty) were rated afterwards, with the model's predictions frozen before rating.

Batch 1 (60 portraits) Batch 2 (100 portraits) Same model on the rater's pool (0.64)

Batch 1 had 30 Black faces and less consistent ratings (repeats 0.69 vs 0.90). Taken together the taste carries over to men at about 0.41 and to Black people at about 0.26, against 0.64 on the rater's own pool.

Did not close the gap: erasing group directions (always worse); a crowd that has seen Black faces (helps its own raters, 0.31 vs 0.25, but agrees with this rater at 0.13); portrait ratings as training data (no weight helped across batches).

9Pre-registered, negatives kept

49
studies, each with its decision rule written before it ran;
6 shaped the final method
  • Zero-shot "attractive" prompts carry almost none of one rater's taste (0.05)
  • Choosing whom to rate next by uncertainty loses to covering the pool
  • Identity directions are not taste directions (0.38 vs 0.46 at 20 ratings)
  • A stronger face model (ArcFace) changed no person's identity

10Limits and responsible use

  • One rater; the pool came from one person's network. Other raters may differ more or less from the crowd.
  • It learns a person's taste including its blind spots (section 8). It is not a measure of anyone's worth.
  • Use only photos you may use; never publish scores or pictures of real people, never use scores to decide about anyone.
